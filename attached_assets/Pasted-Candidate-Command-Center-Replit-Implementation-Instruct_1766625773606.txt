Candidate Command Center
Replit Implementation Instructions (Final, Locked)
This document defines all approved copy, layout, and behavior changes.
Anything not listed here should remain unchanged.
1. HERO GOAL (North Star)
The hero must answer, in under 5 seconds:
What do I get if I use this?
The answer users should immediately understand:
I describe a person → I get ranked, interview-ready candidates with a reason why.
2. HERO COPY (LOCKED)
Headline (unchanged from current)
Get ranked, interview-ready candidates in minutes.
Subline (unchanged from current)
Each result includes why they match — so you know who to contact and what to do next.
Novelty / Power Line (Option A — REQUIRED)
Place directly under the subline, above the search box.
Powered by a new generation of internet-scale people search, now applied to recruiting.
Rules:
One sentence only
No icon
No emphasis styling
Do not repeat this claim anywhere else on the page
3. SEARCH BOX AREA (MOST IMPORTANT CHANGE)
Input placeholder (unchanged)
Describe the person you need, not keywords
PRIMARY CTA PLACEMENT (CHANGE REQUIRED)
The Find Candidates Now button must appear immediately after the input, before example queries.
Reason:
On mobile, examples were competing with the input and confusing users.
CTA restores a clear action hierarchy.
4. “WOW” SCALE CLAIM (NEW, LOCKED LANGUAGE)
This is the approved language to convey scale and capability without jargon.
Approved sentence (use exactly this wording):
Searches across more than a billion public professional profiles — then delivers ranked, interview-ready candidates in minutes.
Placement rules:
Use once
Best location:
Either directly under the CTA helper text, or
In the dark “2 minutes” section
Do not put in the headline
Do not repeat elsewhere
Important:
Do not use the word signal
Do not say “reviews every profile”
Do not reference Exa or benchmarks
5. INPUT HELPER (LOCKED — DO NOT CHANGE)
This line stays exactly as written:
Searches across LinkedIn, GitHub, blogs, portfolios, and live professional activity.
Purpose:
Establishes credibility
Names concrete sources
Should remain human and specific
6. EXAMPLE QUERIES (PLACEMENT + BEHAVIOR CHANGE)
Placement (REQUIRED)
Example queries must appear:
Below the CTA
Visually separated (divider or spacing)
Clearly labeled as optional
Suggested label:
Or try searches like:
Example queries (LOCKED SET)
Limit to 3–4 only. Use these:
Senior engineers in SF who’ve spoken at React conferences
Product managers at fintech companies who write about design systems
Directors of engineering who contribute to open source
ML engineers who publish papers or technical blogs
Behavior rules:
Clicking a query fills the input
Does not auto-search (unless you explicitly choose to)
Examples should look like chips, not input fields
7. LANGUAGE RULES (GLOBAL)
Words to AVOID everywhere:
signal
retrieval
embeddings
synthesis (outside founder narrative)
semantic layer
Preferred language:
looks at
finds
understands
shows you
explains why
work and activity
public professional profiles
If a sentence wouldn’t sound natural spoken out loud to a recruiter, rewrite it.
8. DARK “2 MINUTES” SECTION (OPTIONAL BUT RECOMMENDED)
If you keep this section, the supporting line should reinforce output + scale.
Recommended pairing:
Header
2 minutes per candidate
Subline
Searches across more than a billion public professional profiles — then delivers ranked, interview-ready candidates in minutes.
This creates the “how is this even possible?” moment — cleanly.
9. FINAL UX PRINCIPLE (DO NOT VIOLATE)
The page should assert results, not explain technology
Novelty should feel obvious, not marketed
Output > process > architecture
If something feels wordy, cut explanation before cutting clarity.
